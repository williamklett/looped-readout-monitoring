AI alignment is increasingly a practical safety concern. At the same time, models that reason in latent space can bypass the textual chain of thought used for oversight. Monitoring their internal activations is a natural alternative, but prior work shows that optimization against a monitor can encourage concealment rather than behavioral change. We introduce a monitoring strategy for looped transformers that makes suppressing a monitored signal costly: decode the state after each reasoning pass, then randomly stop and use that same distribution to answer. If the monitored token set contains the correct answer, suppressing its probability necessarily limits correct-answer probability at a random stop. This constraint holds regardless of how the model represents its reasoning. From the resulting expected loss, we derive optimal token-category probabilities for an unrestricted optimizer and test them in two-token addition. Observed probabilities differ from the frozen predictions by 1.68 percentage points on average across the original penalized runs, and 3.73 points including an adaptive lower-penalty extension. Agreement improves substantially during training. The results show how a shared reasoning and output interface can impose a predictable answer-probability cost on readout suppression.
